## 1. Executive Summary

Key findings and actions:
1. **Duration Elasticity Ablation ($c = +0.5972$):** Although multivariate modeling on the duration-bearing subset ($N=561$, China and Maxwell) achieved statistical significance ($p = 8.16 \times 10^{-12}$) and nominal relative MdAPE gain (14.50%), the fitted duration elasticity exponent was strongly positive ($c = +0.5972$). Under positive elasticity, schedule compression (e.g. 12 months $\to$ 6 months) artificially reduces estimated effort, violating Brooks' Law and basic project economics. Under Criterion 3 of the frozen protocol, **duration was rejected from the primary effort prediction equation**. The production point estimate is strictly size-only: $E = a \cdot S^b$. 2. **Team Size Removal:** `team_size` was verified to be unpopulated (0% non-null) in `projects_v2.csv`. It was removed from model inputs (`allowed_inputs: ["size_fp"]`). It remains in API/UI exclusively for FTE cost calculation and timeline feasibility.

   - NLP-derived or defaulted sizes are permanently barred from Data Quality Tier A.

### Pre-Registered Decision Rule Audit

| Criterion | Pre-Registered Threshold | Observed Value | Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Statistical Significance** | Paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank $p < 0.05$ | $p = 8.157 \times 10^{-12}$ | **PASS** |
| **2. Material Accuracy Gain** | Relative Macro MdAPE improvement $\ge 5.0\%$ | $+14.50\%$ | **PASS** |
| **3. Defensible Direction** | Exponent must not allow schedule compression to falsely reduce effort ($c \le 0$) | $c = +0.5972 > 0$ | **FAIL** |
| **Overall Decision** | **All three conditions must PASS** | Condition 3 failed | **REJECT DURATION** |

**Rationale:**
In observational empirical software engineering datasets, larger projects take both longer durations and more effort. Fitting an unconstrained multivariate model $E = a \cdot S^b \cdot D^c$ captures this observational co-movement as a positive elasticity $c \approx 0.60$.
However, when deployed as an interactive estimation tool, this causes an unacceptable failure mode: if an estimator or project manager attempts to compress the schedule (say, requiring delivery in 6 months instead of 12), the model would calculate $E \times (6/12)^{0.60} \approx 0.66 \times E$, falsely implying that rushing a project requires 34% *less* labor.
Brooks' Law and scheduling realities dictate that schedule compression increases risk and effort rather than decreasing it. Because the empirical elasticity cannot be defended in what-if scenarios without complex multi-variable penalty functions that lack empirical grounding, the production point estimate is frozen as **strictly size-only**:
$$E = a \cdot S^b$$
